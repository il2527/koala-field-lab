The real phenomenon

A specialized diet, a selective eater.

Eucalyptus leaves contain natural toxins. Koalas have adaptations for this diet, but they are selective about the leaves they eat.

Joeys develop in their mother’s pouch for about six months, then begin spending time outside it, including riding on her back. The youngest joey is shown using a schematic pouch cutaway. Pap passes digestive microorganisms from mother to joey; older joeys gradually eat leaves while still receiving milk.

The model’s limits

A question, not a proven rule.

The claim that koalas cannot recognize leaves on a flat surface was not verified in the sources below. This prototype applies that assumption automatically whenever the koala reaches the ground.

At zero energy, the mother koala dies in this simplified simulation. This invented energy rule does not predict survival in real animals; a joey at zero is shown as depleted and can recover through stage-appropriate feeding or rest while the mother is alive. Neither bar predicts real animal survival.

Computational thinking & pseudocode

Decompose: leaf placement, energy, mother–joey attachment, and rest. Patterns: repeated searching and selective feeding. Abstract: represent complex metabolism with separate mother and joey scores and a few decision rules.

START mother = 60, joey = 60
SELECT development stage; preserve both scores
FEED MOTHER: accept suitable branch leaves; ignore flat leaves
  mother += food - activity - stage-specific milk and carrying costs
  joey -= 2 (ongoing needs)
FEED JOEY: milk early; milk + pap during transition; leaves when older
  mother pays stage-specific care and activity costs
  joey gains from eligible food; pap explains the digestive transition
ENOUGH REST: both scores increase (simplified recovery)
NOT ENOUGH REST: both scores decrease
Clamp both scores to 0–100
At mother = 0: show death and stop actions until Reset
